11 things Hoard does

OL.library×5

One library

Everything you own from five stores, together. Search it, sort it, filter by store, creator, tag or file type, and see what's new since you last looked.

DF.sync

Downloads that finish

Sync reads each store and downloads anything new, with progress, speed and time left. Stop is safe: a file part way through carries on from where it was next time.

UN.updateUpdate

Updates, noticed

When a creator updates something you've downloaded, Hoard tells you, and fetches only the new files.

SE.set×4

Sets you can share

Group an avatar with its outfits, hair and shaders, even ones you don't own yet. Share it as a link that shows each one and where to buy it, and import the whole set into Unity at once.

LI.unitypackage

Look inside

See the files in a package before you import it, even one inside a zip, and what else it needs: a shader such as Poiyomi or lilToon, or another product you own.

UP.project×2

Your Unity projects

The same list as the Creator Companion and ALCOM. Make a project from VRChat's templates with products from your library in it, update its packages, rename it, and back it up.

GT.gitmain

Source control with Git

Set a project up with Unity's and VRChat's ignore rules, see what changed, commit, and sync with GitHub or your own server. Hoard uses the Git you have, and keeps no passwords.

TH.tagsArchived

Tags, hidden and archived

Tag your assets your way, archive what you're done with, and keep a hidden library behind a PIN.

BK.backupPrevious

Versions, backups and space

Keep the version you had when you update. Find files kept twice, even across stores, and what no project uses. Back up your tags, sets and settings in one file.

SI.signin

Sign in your way

Sign in to each store once, on its own page, with your password, a passkey, or Google, Discord or X. Hoard never sees your password.

YC.yours

Yours, on your computer

No account, no tracking, nothing sent to us. Your sign-ins are encrypted with your computer's own keyring, and everything stays in your folders.

Hoard speaks English, Japanese (日本語) and Korean (한국어): choose in Settings. Its docs do too: 日本語のドキュメント, 한국어 문서.

Hoard for Unity

Everything you've downloaded, inside the Unity editor: Hoard › Open Hoard, in its own menu. Search your library with pictures (animated ones play), see which assets are already in this project, and import one without downloading it again, even from a zip. Import set brings in a whole set at once, the newest version of each package, and each product shows what it needs and whether that's here.

Create Credits List lists the creators of every asset fully in your project, each linked to its public store page, ready to paste into your avatar's description. Hoard's Projects shows each project's assets too.

Editor only: nothing from it goes into your uploads. Unity 2022.3, the version VRChat uses.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11. Installs for you only, with no administrator prompt, and updates itself.

Code-signed. Windows may still say it "protected your PC" while Hoard earns Microsoft's trust: choose More info (the publisher shows as Hoard's maintainer), then Run anyway.

macOS

macOS 11 or newer, on Apple Silicon (M1 and newer) or Intel. Hoard goes in Applications.

It isn't signed by Apple yet: open it, choose Done, then System Settings › Privacy & Security › Open Anyway.

Linux

x86_64: an AppImage, a .deb for Debian and Ubuntu, or a Flatpak.

The Flatpak uses the GNOME runtime from Flathub.

Every file is built on GitHub from this project's code, with a signed record of how, and checked by VirusTotal before it's published. How to install, update and check a download.

Safe by design

  • Your passwords stay yours. You type them into each store's own page, in a browser that belongs to Hoard alone. Hoard keeps only the "stay signed in" pass, encrypted by Windows, your Mac's Keychain or your Linux keyring.
  • Downloads are checked. Files go only where they belong, a download that stops is resumed and verified, and nothing a store sends can land outside your downloads folder.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. Hoard talks only to the stores you use, to GitHub if you turn on update checks, to a photo site when you add a set's photo by its page there, to the notification services you set up (if you do), to the package repositories and Git remotes your projects use, and to Hoard's share server if you make a short link for a set, which it can't read.
  • Open source. MIT licensed, with its code, security reviews and every release on GitHub.
