How Hoard works

Hoard is an app on your computer. It signs in to your stores the way you would, reads what you've bought, downloads it into a folder of yours, and keeps it up to date. There's no Hoard account and no Hoard server: everything happens between your computer and your stores.

Step by step

  1. It runs on your computer

    Hoard's window shows pages that Hoard itself serves, to your computer only. Other websites can't reach them, and your other devices only can if you switch that on. Your library, settings and sign-ins are kept in Hoard's own folder on your computer, and your downloads in a folder you choose.

  2. You sign in on each store's own page

    Each store opens in a browser window of its own, and you sign in there, with your password, Google, Discord or X, just as you would anywhere. Hoard never sees your password. It keeps only the "stay signed in" pass the store hands out, locked by your Windows account, your Mac's Keychain or your Linux keyring, and each store's pass is kept apart from the others. itch.io uses an API key instead, which you make on itch.io, as its own app does.

  3. It reads what you own

    Refresh reads each store's library the way you'd see it: Booth, Gumroad, Jinxxy and Payhip in their signed-in windows, itch.io through its API. Rather not sign in somewhere? Save that store's library pages from your usual browser and import them instead. Everything goes into one library, where Hoard suggests tags from the products' names and notices the same product bought on two stores.

  4. It downloads, and finishes

    Sync downloads anything you don't have yet, straight from the store over secure connections, with progress, speed and time left. A file that's cut off carries on from where it was next time, and Stop is always safe. Your sign-in is only ever sent to that store, never to the servers that host its files. Jobs wait their turn in Tasks, and Hoard can carry on in the background while its window is closed. Payhip is the exception: Hoard lists what you bought there, and you download it from Payhip.

  5. It keeps things up to date

    When a creator adds or changes a file, the next sync, or Check for updates, notices, and fetches only what's new. Once a week (or when you choose), Hoard checks that the files on your disk are still the ones it downloaded, and says if any went missing or changed. A full drive? Add a library folder on another one, and Hoard reads it as part of your library.

  6. It brings them into Unity

    Hoard writes a catalog of everything it's downloaded, and seals it so changes made by anything else are noticed. Hoard for Unity reads that catalog in the editor: search your assets with their pictures, see which are already in the open project, and import one through Unity's own import dialog, without downloading it again.

What stays with you

  • Nothing is sent to us. No accounts, no tracking, no analytics, no ads. Hoard talks only to the stores you use, and to GitHub if you turn on update checks.
  • Your files are ordinary files. One folder per store, creator and product, readable without Hoard. Updating or uninstalling Hoard never touches them.
  • Support reports are made on your computer. Passwords, cookies, keys and names are taken out, and Hoard never sends a report anywhere: you choose whether to share it.
  • It works offline for everything except signing in, refreshing and downloading.

Want the details? The help pages go through every part of Hoard, SECURITY.md says exactly how it keeps you safe, and ARCHITECTURE.md explains how it's built.
