# Hoard's videos

The promos ([#273](https://github.com/Soloflighter1010/Hoard-Asset-Manager/issues/273)) and the help videos
([#274](https://github.com/Soloflighter1010/Hoard-Asset-Manager/issues/274)) are made here, and so is their music
([#291](https://github.com/Soloflighter1010/Hoard-Asset-Manager/issues/291)). Nothing in them is generated by a model:
the pictures are Hoard's own screens, taken by `scripts/screenshots.py`, and shapes drawn in HTML and SVG (the Heap
as `hoard/heap.py` draws it everywhere); the music is notes written out in `music/loop.py` and synthesised there.

| | |
|---|---|
| `films/` | One file a video: its scenes, each a number of bars long, with its words in English, Japanese and Korean |
| `video/film.html` | Draws a film, one frame at a time |
| `build.py` | Takes a picture of every frame, checks it, and puts the video together with the music |
| `video/still.html` | Draws a still: a video's thumbnail, or a poster |
| `thumbs.py` | Each video's thumbnail, for YouTube: drawn, checked and saved |
| `posters.json`, `posters.py` | The posters (square, for VRChat's poster frames and social sites): drawn, checked and saved |
| `music/loop.py` | The music: a loop of 32 bars with no seam, and the chord a video ends on |
| `regions.json` | Things on Hoard's pages a film can point at, by name, found again in every language |
| `requirements.txt` | What these need beyond Hoard's own requirements: numpy, SciPy and Pillow, hash-checked |

## Making them

You need Python 3.11 or later (Pillow 13 does), Hoard's requirements and these
(`pip install --require-hashes -r requirements.txt -r promo/requirements.txt`),
Playwright's Chromium (`python -m playwright install chromium`), and ffmpeg.

```
python scripts/screenshots.py PRODUCTS_FOLDER build/shots --themes=dark,light --lang=en --regions=promo/regions.json
python scripts/screenshots.py PRODUCTS_FOLDER build/shots --themes=dark,light --lang=ja --regions=promo/regions.json
python scripts/screenshots.py PRODUCTS_FOLDER build/shots --themes=dark,light --lang=ko --regions=promo/regions.json
python promo/music/loop.py
python promo/build.py promo-short promo-long help-booth-signed-out ... --lang=en,ja,ko --shape=landscape,portrait
python promo/thumbs.py
python promo/posters.py
```

## The videos

| Film | Length | Shapes |
|---|---|---|
| `promo-short` | 40 seconds: the Library, downloads and updates, sets and wishlists | landscape (1920 × 1080), portrait (1080 × 1920) |
| `promo-long` | 2 minutes: every part of Hoard, each explained | landscape, portrait |
| `help-…` | under a minute each: one problem the wiki's Troubleshooting page explains, and its fix | landscape |

There's a help video for each problem in issue #274: `help-windows-warning`, `help-social-signin`,
`help-booth-signed-out`, `help-payhip-check`, `help-itch-key`, `help-download-fails`, `help-notifications`,
`help-unrecognised-page` and `help-hidden-pin`. Each is linked from the wiki section that explains its problem
(Troubleshooting, and the FAQ), in each language, at its address on the `media` release:
`https://github.com/Soloflighter1010/Hoard-Asset-Manager/releases/download/media/hoard-<film>-<lang>-<shape>.mp4`.
A help video says what its wiki section says: change both together.

`PRODUCTS_FOLDER` is the products the website's screenshots show, the ones listed on its
[credits page](https://hoard.furryup.link/credits.html) (`scripts/export_screenshot_products.py` makes such a folder
from a library); the Media workflow in Actions takes them from `promo/products`. Only those products are shown,
with their own pictures. The videos land in `build/promo/out/`, named `hoard-<film>-<lang>-<shape>.mp4`; a film is
made only in the languages it has its words in.

`python promo/build.py FILM --preview=2,9.5,14` takes pictures of a few moments instead, to look at while writing a
film. Each frame is checked as it's drawn: a highlight or a picture that would run off the frame, or out of its window,
stops the build and says where.

## The thumbnails

`promo/thumbs.py` makes a thumbnail for each video, named like it: `build/promo/thumbs/hoard-<film>-<lang>-<shape>.jpg`.
A landscape one is 1280 × 720, YouTube's size for a video's thumbnail; a portrait one is 1080 × 1920, for a Short.
Each is under 2 MB, YouTube's limit. They're drawn as the videos are, from the same screens, the Heap and words, by
`video/still.html`, from each film's `"thumb"`:

- `"look": "short"`: the title, the stores, Projects and your own files, and the Heap over Hoard's library (`"image"`).
- `"look": "tour"`: the title, what Hoard does (`"features"`, the long promo's own words) and a fan of Hoard's
  screens (`"images"`, the first at the back).
- `"look": "help"`: the problem (`"title"`, short), **How to fix it**, and a lens close in on one of the video's
  steps (`"scene"`): on a Hoard screen, its first focus (or `"focus"`), at the screen's own size or closer; on a
  drawn window, its state `"state"`, with what's clicked in it lit.

Each is checked as it's drawn: a title too long for its room, anything off the picture or under the lens, or a lens
that cuts off what it's about, and it isn't made (`--draft` makes it anyway, to look at). A title is as large as it
fits, in at most three lines; a Japanese one breaks only between its phrases.

## The posters

`promo/posters.py` makes the posters in `posters.json`, in each language: `build/promo/posters/hoard-poster-<name>-<lang>.jpg`,
2048 × 2048 (the most VRChat's poster frames load, and fine for social sites). Their words are the films' own, so
they say things the way the videos do, in every language:

- `"look": "hero"`: the Heap and Hoard's name, what it is, the stores (and Projects, and your own files), and where to
  get it: the short promo's opening and end card.
- `"look": "feature"`: one of the long promo's screens (`"scene"`), with its name, title and words, the screen with
  `"box"` ringed, and a lens on `"lens"` (a thing on the screen, or several together; `null`: none), placed where it
  covers neither the ring nor the words.

The fine print says whose the products shown are, and that Hoard isn't VRChat's or the stores'. Each is checked as
the thumbnails are (`--draft` makes one anyway).

## Writing a film

The music is 100 beats a minute, so a bar is 2.4 seconds; every scene is a whole or half number of bars long, so the
cuts land on the beat. A film says which bar of the loop it starts on (`music.start_bar`); its end card gets the
ending chord.

A screen's scene points at things on it by their names in `regions.json` (`"focus": [[beat, "name", padding,
"what the lens shows"]]`): a ring goes round it, and a lens shows it up close, whole (`--regions` saves the box of all
an element draws, what sticks out of it too), with a little of the screen round it fading out, so nothing ends in a
hard cut. A few screens are taken for the videos alone (`wishlist-prices`, `downloads-files`), scrolled so what
they look at closely isn't cut off by the page's edge. `tests/test_promo.py` checks every name a film uses is there.

A help video's step outside Hoard (a store's page, Windows' warning, a Save as dialog, a terminal) is a plain drawing
(`"type": "hwindow"`, with its words under `"win"`: a browser's page, a dialog, a search for an app or a terminal),
clicked through by the pointer: never a picture of anyone's own page or design. `scripts/screenshots.py --regions`
takes the screens the videos alone use after the website's (its `VIDEO_SHOTS`): Hoard as it is in each help video's
problem, and the parts the long promo shows, Hoard's looks among them.

Only made-up data is shown: a data folder `scripts/screenshots.py` makes, never yours, with the products given.
